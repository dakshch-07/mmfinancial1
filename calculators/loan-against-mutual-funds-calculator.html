How Does Loan Against Mutual Funds (LAMF) Work?

How to Use the Loan Against Mutual Funds Calculator?

Using the calculator is simple. You'll just need a few details about your mutual fund holdings:

Mutual Fund Name

Type the name of your mutual fund scheme: equity, debt, or hybrid. The calculator fetches the latest NAV (Net Asset Value) directly from official AMC data.

Number of Units

Enter the number of units you hold in that fund or the specific number of units you plan to pledge as loan collateral.

Once you enter your mutual fund scheme name(s) and number of units, the calculator instantly gives you a clear picture of your borrowing potential:

📈 NAV (Net Asset Value)

This is the previous working day's per-unit closing price of your mutual fund. We ensure your loan estimate is as accurate as possible. NAVs are updated daily by AMCs.

💰 Total Value per scheme = NAV × Units

This is the current market value of your mutual fund holdings. For example, if the NAV is ₹50 and you hold 1,000 units, your total value is ₹50,000.

⚖️ LTV % (Loan-to-Value Ratio)

The LTV is the percentage of your mutual fund's value that you can get as a loan. Equity funds have an LTV of up to 45-50%, while Debt funds have an LTV of up to 80-85%. The calculator automatically uses the applicable LTV based on the type of mutual fund.

🎯 Eligible Amount per scheme

Based on the total value and LTV, the calculator shows how much loan you can get against your mutual fund schemes. Continuing the above example: ₹50,000 total value × 45% LTV = ₹22,500 eligible loan amount.

🏦 Total Loan Amount (Credit Limit)

The total eligible loan calculated by summing up the loan amounts from each mutual fund scheme you've added (split cleanly into Equity and Debt accounts).

📅 Monthly & Daily Interest

Interest is charged strictly on the amount actually used, and only for the exact number of days it is utilised at your chosen interest rate (e.g. 10.25% - 10.50% p.a.).

How Loan Amount Changes with Investments?

The higher the value of your pledged investments, the higher your loan amount. It depends on:

1. Type of Mutual Fund (Equity / Debt / Hybrid)
2. NAV (Which changes daily)
3. No. of Units Held (Units marked for lien)
4. Applicable LTV (45% Equity / 80% Debt)

What is a Loan Against Mutual Funds?

A Loan Against Mutual Funds (LAMF) is an overdraft credit facility that enables investors to leverage their existing mutual fund holdings as collateral without selling or liquidating their investment portfolio. Through a secure, paperless digital lien-marking mechanism with registrars such as CAMS and KFintech, your mutual fund units remain safely invested in your name while an authorized lender sanctions a dedicated credit line.

Unlike conventional personal loans where fixed EMIs commence immediately on the full disbursed sum, LAMF operates on an as-you-use basis. You only pay interest on the specific rupee balance drawn down, calculated on a daily basis. The unutilized portion incurs zero interest charges, making it one of the most flexible emergency liquidity solutions in India.

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Search & Select Schemes: Type at least three characters of your mutual fund scheme name. Select your exact growth or regular plan to fetch the latest official NAV.
  2. Input Units Held: Enter the quantity of units from your latest mutual fund statement (CAS). The calculator automatically figures the gross investment value.
  3. Verify Category & LTV: Equity-oriented funds default to 45% LTV, while debt and liquid funds default to 80% LTV. You can adjust the LTV within approved lender boundaries.
  4. Adjust Overdraft Usage & Stress Test: Move the utilization slider to determine exact monthly and daily interest on actual borrowings, and simulate market crashes using the stress-test slider.

How is the Loan Amount Calculated? (Worked Example)

Lenders evaluate your credit capacity by multiplying your gross holding value by the regulatory Loan-to-Value (LTV) ratio assigned to that asset class. Equity funds carry conservative limits to cushion market swings, while debt funds permit substantially higher borrowing.

Why Choose LAMF Instead of Redeeming Mutual Funds?

  • Unbroken Compounding: Your units remain active in the fund. Any capital appreciation, dividend reinvestments, and compounding benefits continue uninterrupted.
  • Zero Capital Gains Tax: Pledging units is not a sale. Consequently, you incur zero short-term or long-term capital gains tax liabilities.
  • Interest Only on Actual Drawdown: Borrow ₹50,000 from a ₹5,00,000 sanctioned limit, and interest is billed exclusively on ₹50,000 for the duration used.
  • Flexible Principal Repayment: No rigid EMI penalty. Repay whenever surplus liquidity is generated, with zero prepayment charges.
  • Important Risk Consideration: Pledged portfolios are revalued daily against closing NAVs. If a sharp market decline causes your limit to drop below your drawn balance, your lender may request additional collateral or a partial repayment to rebalance margins.
